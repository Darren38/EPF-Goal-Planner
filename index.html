How to use this calculator
1

Set your starting point

Enter your current EPF balance, the date you want to count from, and your age today. Age matters: contribution rates change at 60, and it decides whether the goal lands before or after you can withdraw.

2

Pick a goal

Type any amount, or use a chip. The three benchmark chips are EPF's own retirement adequacy targets by age 60: Basic, Adequate and Enhanced.

3

Choose where the money comes from

From my salary splits the contribution into your share and your employer's. Fixed amount is for anyone paying in themselves, such as i-Saraan or voluntary contributions, where there is no employer share.

4

Let the statutory rates follow you

Tick Use statutory rates and both percentages follow the schedule for your member type: 11% and 13% up to RM5,000 of wages, 12% above, 2% each for non-Malaysians, and 0% and 4% once you turn 60. The band is re-checked every projected year, so a raise past RM5,000 drops the employer rate on its own.

5

Add a bonus or a top-up

The yearly extra is a single payment, not a monthly one. A bonus counts as wages, so both sides contribute on it. A voluntary top-up is entirely your own money. Pick the month it lands in: January earns almost a full year of dividend, December earns none.

6

Pick a dividend basis

Fixed uses one rate you choose. Historical average uses the mean of past declared rates. Trend line fits a line to history. Replay history loops real past rates forward so you can test a bad run.

7

Read the answer

The big number is how long it takes. The tiles give the date, your age then, the split between your money, your employer's and dividends, and what the balance is worth in today's ringgit.

8

Compare and adjust

Every field has a slider. If you changed one thing runs the whole projection four more times, each with a single change, so you can see which one actually buys you the most time and the most money.

Year by year

One row per calendar year, because EPF pays its dividend once a year on 31 December. The first row is usually a part year running from your start date to the end of that year. The last row stops on the month the goal is crossed, so it shows no dividend if the crossing happened on a contribution. The highlighted row is when the goal is reached.

How this is worked out

EPF style, cycle of n months ending 31 December
D = r × [ B(n ÷ 12) + C × n(n − 1) ÷ 24 + Σ Lk(12 − mk) ÷ 12 ] Bnext = B + nC + Σ Lk + D
Monthly compounding, for comparison
Bm+1 = Bm(1 + i) + C + Lm i = r ÷ 12

The 5.5 factor

For a full year n = 12 and the contribution term becomes 5.5C. Contributions arrive month by month rather than all at once: a payment in month m earns dividend for (12 − m) months, and those fractions add up to 5.5 months across the twelve payments.

A yearly extra, the L term

L is a bonus, a top-up or an i-Saraan incentive paid once in month m, so it earns (12 − m) ÷ 12 of a year's dividend: almost a full year in January, nothing in December. It only lands in a cycle if that month falls inside it, so starting in July with a January bonus means the first bonus arrives the following calendar year.

Why the start date matters

The first cycle runs from your start date to 31 December, so it is usually a part year. Starting in January earns you almost a full year of dividend on that first stretch; starting in November earns you almost none. The same inputs can shift the finish by close to a year.

Crossing on 31 December

When the goal is crossed by the annual dividend rather than by a contribution, the exact day is 31 December. EPF declares and credits the dividend a few months later and backdates it, so treat that as the accounting date, not the day the money appears.

Rates are declared, not forecast

Dividend rates are set after the fact and are guaranteed by law to be at least 2.5% for Simpanan Konvensional. Simpanan Shariah carries no such floor: it pays what the shariah portfolio earns. Treat any single figure as a scenario, not a promise.

Statutory contribution rates

Malaysian members under 60 contribute 11%, employers 13% on wages up to RM5,000 and 12% above. From 60 the member pays 0% and the employer 4%. Non-Malaysian members contribute 2% and 2% since October 2025. Every band is retested each projected year, so a rising salary or a birthday can change the rate mid-projection.

What counts as wages

EPF wages include salary, bonus, commission, allowances and paid leave. They exclude overtime, service charges, gratuity, retirement and retrenchment benefits, travelling allowances and director's fees. Enter the wage figure, not your gross package.

Bonus counts as wages

Both sides contribute on a bonus, at the same rates as salary. The statutory band is set by your base wage, so a bonus that pushes a single month above RM5,000 does not by itself drop the employer rate from 13% to 12%. A voluntary top-up is your own money and carries no employer share.

The three accounts

Since 11 May 2024 new contributions are split 75% to Akaun Persaraan, 15% to Akaun Sejahtera and 10% to Akaun Fleksibel. All three earn the same dividend. Persaraan is locked until 55, Sejahtera is for housing, health and education, and Fleksibel can be withdrawn at any time. Set how your existing balance is split; the default assumes the same shares.

Third Schedule rounding

EPF officially computes contributions from the Third Schedule, which uses wage bands and rounds to set amounts. Straight percentages are used here, so a payslip may differ by a few ringgit a month. Above RM20,000 a month, exact percentages apply anyway.

Inflation and today's money

The projection is entirely in future ringgit. The today's money figure discounts the ending balance back at the inflation rate you set, which is the honest way to read a target set decades out. It changes nothing in the projection itself.

What is still not modelled

Withdrawals of any kind, the RM100,000 a year cap on self contributions, tax relief, the separate account dividend timing, and the death or incapacitation benefits. Nor does it decide whether the goal is the right one for you.
