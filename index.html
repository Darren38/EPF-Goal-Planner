How to use this calculator
1

Set your starting point

Enter your current EPF balance and the date you want to count from. Everything else builds on those two.

2

Add a bonus or a top-up

The yearly extra is a single payment, not a monthly one. A bonus counts as wages, so both you and your employer contribute on it. A voluntary top-up is entirely your own money. Pick the month it lands in, because a payment in January earns almost a full year of dividend and one in December earns none.

3

Choose where the money comes from

From my salary splits the contribution into your share and your employer's: enter your monthly wage, then the percentage each side pays. Fixed amount is for anyone paying in themselves, such as i-Saraan or voluntary contributions, where there is no employer share.

4

Enter both percentages

Type your own share and your employer's share directly. The statutory figures are noted under each field. If you would rather not track the wage band yourself, tick Set this for me and the employer share follows 13% up to RM5,000 and 12% above, re-checked each year so a raise past the threshold drops it on its own.

5

Pick a dividend basis

Fixed uses one rate you choose. Historical average uses the mean of past declared rates. Trend line fits a line to history. Replay history loops real past rates forward so you can test a bad run.

6

Read the answer

The big number is how long it takes. The Target reached tile gives the date, and you can show it as an exact day, a month, or just a year.

7

Read the chart

Each bar is one calendar year. Hover any bar for its closing balance. The first bar is usually a part year running from your start date to 31 December.

8

Adjust and compare

Every field has a slider. Drag one and everything updates instantly, so you can see how much a bigger contribution actually buys you.

Two things to keep in mind. EPF pays its dividend once a year on 31 December, so if the target is crossed by dividend rather than by a contribution, the date will always land on 31 December. And no rate here is a forecast: declared rates are set after the fact and have swung between 4.25% and 6.90% in the last 25 years. Use the modes to bracket a range, not to pin down a date.

On the contribution figures. EPF officially calculates contributions from the Third Schedule, which uses wage bands and rounds to set amounts rather than applying a clean percentage. Straight percentages are used here, so your actual payslip may differ by a few ringgit a month. Rates also differ for members aged 60 and above; untick the automatic employer rate and enter your own if that applies to you.

How this is worked out

EPF style, cycle of n months ending 31 December
D = r × [ B(n ÷ 12) + C × n(n − 1) ÷ 24 + L(12 − m) ÷ 12 ] Bnext = B + nC + L + D
Monthly compounding, closed form
FV = P(1 + i)n + C × [(1 + i)n − 1] ÷ i i = r ÷ 12

The 5.5 factor

For a full year n = 12 and the contribution term becomes 5.5C. Contributions arrive month by month rather than all at once: a payment in month m earns dividend for (12 − m) months, and those fractions add up to 5.5 months across the twelve payments.

A yearly extra, the L term

L is a bonus or top-up paid once in month m, so it earns (12 − m) ÷ 12 of a year's dividend: almost a full year in January, nothing in December. It only lands in a cycle if that month falls inside it, so starting in July with a January bonus means the first bonus arrives the following calendar year.

Why the start date matters

The first cycle runs from your start date to 31 December, so it is usually a part year. Starting in January gives a near full stub year of dividend, starting in November gives almost none. The same inputs can shift the finish by close to a year.

Crossing on 31 December

When the goal is crossed by the annual dividend rather than by a contribution, the exact day is 31 December. EPF declares and credits the dividend a few months later and backdates it, so treat that as the accounting date, not the day the money appears.

Rates are declared, not forecast

Dividend rates are set after the fact. Recent declared rates for conventional savings have sat roughly between 5.0% and 6.3%, and the ten year mean is close to 5.9%. Treat any single figure as a scenario.

Statutory contribution rates

Malaysian members under 60 contribute 11%, employers 13% on wages up to RM5,000 and 12% above. Non-Malaysian members have contributed 2% and 2% since October 2025. The employer band is retested each projected year, so a rising salary can cross the threshold mid-projection.

Bonus counts as wages

Both sides contribute on a bonus, at the same rates as salary. The statutory band is set by your base wage, so a bonus that pushes a single month above RM5,000 does not by itself drop the employer rate from 13% to 12%. A voluntary top-up is your own money and carries no employer share.

Third Schedule rounding

EPF officially computes contributions from the Third Schedule, which uses wage bands and rounds to set amounts. Straight percentages are used here, so a payslip may differ by a few ringgit a month. Above RM20,000 a month, exact percentages apply anyway.

What is not modelled

Account 1, 2 and 3 splits, withdrawals, age 60 and above rates, and the separate shariah dividend when it diverges. Neither is inflation, so whatever goal you set will buy less in the future than it does today.
